# Lithe

The Lithe site: the walk drawing, then the essay, *Scaling the Human Experience: An Engine to Fight Brute Force*, then the founders and contact.

While the page loads, a white screen shows "move" in braille: one cell at the centre, the letters m, o, v and e in turn in the same place, each dot a small disc in a fine ordered dither, the dots of one letter dissolving into the next through the same dither (`dist/braille.js`). It spells the word at least once (under a second), then the white screen fades quickly away (0.3 s) as soon as the page has loaded. It only shows with scripts and full motion, and an inline gate in the page's head removes it after eight seconds whatever happens.

The page opens on the walk drawing (generated, below), running the full width at its own size, never cropped: its lines come in off one edge and go out the other, and it ends on its lowest line. It is short enough that the essay title shows under it on load. Screens taller than 2:3 get a version drawn for them. Muybridge's walking plate sits over the drawing's centre, as supplied, in a thin clearing the pencil leaves around it: each line lifts off a little short of the plate, each at its own distance, and starts again past it. The plate walks as you scroll (`dist/plate.js`).

The essay follows in one column centred under the title, its text left-aligned, the page's side margin its only indent, set a little small and dense (leading 1.42; 1.5 on phones). Every paragraph is in one format; the one emphasised line, “A powered exoskeleton is the data engine to solve the embodiment problem.”, is set larger, bold and tighter, and centred, always in the same three lines (a 16em measure). Through it runs the nexus (`dist/assets/nexus.webp`, generated, below): lines that come in wide from the left edge of the screen, gather into the statement through a thin neck, stop just short of its words as a pencil would, and leave on the right to spread wide again, like an hourglass on its side. It is drawn in the statement's em, so it fits its words at every size, and the essay clips it at the screen's edges so it never widens the page. The nexus is written in braille: its lines are set as a braille halftone in 3 px dots, on the grid of a braille page (dots two apart within a cell, cells five dots apart across and seven down), each of a cell's six dots raised where a line runs under it, against an ordered threshold, so every strand is written out in real braille cells following its curve, each cell in its line's ink. The drawing's own lines aren't shown: the braille alone draws them (the drawing stays in the page, unseen, to give the braille its shape and place). As the pointer passes near, a cell turns to another of the same weight, a dot at a time, and settles back once it has gone (`dist/braille.js`). Phones have a nexus of their own (`dist/assets/nexus-tall.webp`, below): there the statement is set in the screen's width, its three lines about three quarters of it, and the lines meet its whole height at its ends and pass over and under its corners, so the hourglass opens round it to the screen's edges, its braille in 2 px dots (as fine, for the statement's size, as the 3 px dots on a large screen), with room above and below it. The page ends on small black tabs flush to its foot over a sliver of the drawing rising from the bottom edge, whose top line draws in from the left as you scroll down to it, its tip thinning into grain (`dist/lines.js`): Lithe 2026 on the left (no ©: the name isn't a registered mark), Product and Manifesto in the middle (Product opens the product page, below; Manifesto goes to the top of the home page), and Waitlist, X and Email on the right. Those three have no link yet: clicked, each shows a small “Coming soon” just above it for a moment, a bordered box over a dithered shadow (`dist/soon.js`). The link tabs invert on hover and keyboard focus (white ground, black text, no transition). On phones Lithe 2026 is dropped and Product and Manifesto move to the left end of the same line. In the dip of that drawing sit the founders: “Founders:”, then a card for each, linking to their LinkedIn. A card is a 1 px black border round white, holding the name, a one-line note (Andre: “Obsessed with design, physical and digital.” Nigel: “Ex Jane-Street and influencer.”), and the portrait standing on its bottom edge, cut out over a layered dither (pink bands echoing the drawing, gathered round the shoulders, with a black layer a pixel out of register; `dist/founders.js`). Under each card is a fine dithered shadow (one pixel in four), 5 px down and to the right; on hover the card lifts a pixel off it and the name underlines. On phones the drawing at the foot is shown 2.6 times the screen's width with its dip at the centre, so the founders still rest in it, side by side, their label above them. There is no navigation.

The product page (`dist/product.html`) is empty but for the exoskeleton's silhouette at rest, arms hanging relaxed: the real v3 CAD render seen from behind (`~/Documents/EXO/output/exo-site-assets/exo-standing-poster.png`), cropped to its edges as `dist/assets/exo.webp`, centred on white, with one small grey line under it: “Human augmentation Suit 1: Coming soon.”. Under the silhouette is the site's shadow, a fine dither (one pixel in four) 5 px down and to the right, cut to its shape with a CSS mask, printed like a two-ink riso: a pink dither layer 2 px further out of register under the black. It has no drawings (those stay on the manifesto). The page ends on the home page's tabs, with no drawing behind them.

Type is IBM Plex Sans, made to express “the relationship between mankind and machine”, the face IBM chose to replace Helvetica Neue; it renders the same on every device, with Helvetica Neue standing in until it loads (the loader hides the swap). One scale, three weights, each with one job: the title in Medium, large, tracking −0.035em, leading 1; the statement in Bold, tracking −0.03em, leading 1.08; the text in Regular, tracking 0, leading 1.42; labels (tabs, names) in Medium at 13 px, tracking +0.01em, and the founders' notes in Regular at 11 px. The font is served from the site (`dist/fonts/`, under the SIL Open Font License in `dist/fonts/OFL.txt`): one variable file for the upright weights and one for the italic, each in Latin and Latin Extended, so a browser fetches only what the page's characters need (59 KB for the Latin pair). Every value that shapes the page is a custom property in `dist/style.css`, with the site's own as its default, so debug mode (below) can tune it. Two inks, black and brand pink (`#ec7aa4`), on white; a very light pink (`#f8cbda`) is used only for text selection. An earlier annual-report layout is archived in `~/Documents/EXO/exo-site-archive/2026-10-05-annual-report/`.

## Debug mode

Add `?debug` to the address (`http://localhost:4188/?debug`) for a panel, top right, that tunes the whole page live (`dist/debug.js`). Without `?debug` none of it runs, and nothing it does changes the site.

- **Presets**: ten combinations, on keys 1–9 and 0: Lithe (the site as it is), Report (IBM's report: light Plex in Gray and Blue), Engine (Big Shoulders in International Orange), Faust (blackletter in ember on black), Riso (expanded Anybody in fluorescent pink and federal blue), Telemetry (Martian Mono in phosphor green), Blueprint (hairline Plex on blue), Klein (Syne in Klein Blue), Manuscript (blackletter and oxblood on bone) and Monolith (condensed black capitals, no colour). Random picks a random pack and palette; Reset goes back to the site; Replay loader plays the loader again.
- **Type**: six font packs (the site's Plex, and five chosen for the company's Faustian ethos: Anybody, a single face from ultra-condensed to ultra-expanded, for will; Grenze Gotisch over Grenze, a modern blackletter, for the Gothic soul; Martian Mono, a monospace superfamily, for the machine; Big Shoulders, Chicago steel, for industry; Syne, for the avant-garde), each fitted so the statement keeps its three lines. Then the size, weight, width, tracking, leading and capitals of the title, the statement (with its measure and alignment), the text and the labels.
- **Colour**: ten palettes (Lithe, Riso, IBM, Signal, Klein, Oxblood, Faust, Blueprint, Phosphor, Mono), or any three inks (paper, ink, accent). The drawings are printed in the site's three inks, so under any others the panel repaints them, pixel by pixel, and swaps them in; the braille, the trace on the plate, the foot line and the portraits' dither follow too. The Muybridge plate and the photographs are never changed.
- **Space**, **Drawings**, **Founders** and **Motion**: margins, the space round the title and the statement, the column width and paragraph gap, the space before the foot; the cover's ink, the plate (on or off, and its size), the nexus (on or off, its pencil lines, and its width), the braille (on or off, density, dot size, ink), the foot drawing and the portraits' dither; the portraits' size, the cards' padding and shadow; the plate's scroll per frame and the loader's pace.
- Double-click a control's name to reset it; a pack or palette clears the values it owns. The backtick key shows or hides the panel. Settings are kept in the browser while debugging; **Settings → Copy** gives them as JSON (send them to bake a look into the site), and pasting settings there and pressing Apply loads them. `?preset=<name>` opens straight into a preset, `?type=<pack>` and `?palette=<palette>` into a pack or palette, and `&tune=closed` with the panel closed.
- Two caveats: the pencil's clearing round the plate is drawn for its size on the site, so a different plate size leaves lines under it or a gap; and the font packs load from Google Fonts.

## Assets and behavior

- The walk drawing (`dist/assets/walk.webp`, 2800 × 1169, and `walk-tall.webp`, 1200 × 1231) is generated in `plates/`. A sagittal walking gait is built from normative hip, knee and ankle angle curves (`plates/gait.js`), with the stance foot planted. The paths traced by points along each limb over three strides are laid along a curve across the frame and echoed above the head (a pink ribbon, then ridges closing into a black field) and below the ground (pale lines). Each band is printed like a two-ink riso (`plates/riso.js`): solid at one edge and thinning to grain, black over pink, the pink slightly out of register. On the sky and ground echoes, the part of each band that thins out is printed in ordered (Bayer) dither instead of grain (`tail: 'dither'`), tying the drawing to the dithered data figure; the solid edges stay grainy, like pencil. With `line: {family, band}`, one band is split off into `<name>-line.webp` on a transparent ground, cut to the same rows as `<name>.webp` (the drawing without it); stacked, the two are pixel for pixel the full drawing, and the page draws the line in. `avoid` keeps the pencil off the rectangle where the Muybridge plate sits on the page, so if the plate's size or the drawing's height changes, its rect must move with it (the numbers are noted in `plates/walk.js` and `walk-tall.js`). With `extend`, every line carries on past both ends of the curve, so it runs off the canvas instead of stopping inside it; with `trim`, the canvas is cut just below the lowest line. The sliver at the foot (`dist/assets/foot.webp`, with its top line in `foot-line.webp`) comes from `plates/foot.js` on a transparent ground. Each drawing's spec is a call in `plates/<name>.js` (`silicon.js` is a spare, unused). To regenerate, run `node plates/render.mjs` from `exo-site/` (Node 22+, Brave or Chrome, and `cwebp` from Homebrew's webp); renders are seeded, so they come out the same each time.
- The nexus (`dist/assets/nexus.webp`, 4000 × 1760 on a transparent ground, 80 px to the statement's em) comes from `plates/nexus.js`: one family of lines echoing the walk, laid along a straight river whose height (`span`) is set at each distance from the middle, thin (0.3em either side) where it meets the words and opening slowly, then faster, toward the screen's edges. Many narrow strokes are laid out and only a sparse, uneven few are inked, each with its own weight, sometimes black, drifting a little on its own (`wobble`), so the spacing is never even. `avoid` with the statement's three line boxes (`rects`) makes the pencil lift short of the words, and `ends` makes every line start and stop on its own near the left and right edges.
- The phone nexus (`dist/assets/nexus-tall.webp`, 1920 × 1120, 24 × 14 em at the same 80 px to the em) comes from `plates/nexus-tall.js`: the same lines and line boxes on a river that is already as tall as the statement at the middle and opens to about 4.3em either side at a 390 px screen's edges, so the outer lines pass over and under the statement's corners and the pencil lifts just short of them. The page picks it with a `<source>` for screens up to 600 px; `braille.js` reads it at 216 samples across (`data-samples`) rather than 500, so each sample is the same share of the finer dots' spacing.
- The Muybridge plate walks on scroll (`dist/plate.js`). At the top of the page it is shown untouched. Every 24 px of scroll, each of the twelve boxes shows the next frame, so the sequence plays in all twelve at once, each at its own phase. A second canvas carries a faint motion-capture trace that cuts in on the first scroll, with no fade, and moves with the frames. It leads with the feet: an ankle marker on each foot, a pink stride line between them, a dotted trail from the swing foot's previous position, and its coordinates in a 3 × 5 pixel font. The head gets a lighter marker. Under reduced motion the frames hold still.
- The founders' portraits (`dist/assets/andre.webp`, `nigel.webp`, 600 × 600, transparent) are cut from the CUPI site's team photos (`~/Documents/CUPI/Website-CUPI/photos/full/`): each square crop is 2.6 face widths with the eyes 42% down, so the heads match. The hair is matted with the macOS Vision framework's person segmentation (accurate), bounded by its subject mask so no background is taken for hair, and in the soft edge each pixel's colour is solved free of the background behind it (estimated from the clear background nearby), so no trace of the auditorium is left in the strands. On Andre's photo a dark band of background behind his hair tips, left of the jaw, is faded out by hand (`cutBelow`). To rebuild them, run `node portraits/make.mjs` from `exo-site/` (macOS for the Vision framework, Node 22+, Brave or Chrome, and `dwebp` / `cwebp` from Homebrew's webp); the crops and the hand fade are set at the top of that file.
- Link previews use `dist/assets/og.jpg` (1200 × 630), rendered from `og-card.html`: the page as it opens, the walk drawing across the card with the Muybridge plate in its clearing and the title under it in Plex. To re-render it, screenshot that file at 1200 × 630 in headless Brave or Chrome once its font has loaded, and encode it as a JPEG under 300 KB (WhatsApp skips larger previews), e.g. `cjpeg -quality 86 -sample 1x1 -optimize -progressive`. Bump the `?v=` on `og:image` so chat apps fetch it again.
- Native page scrolling works with touch, trackpads, wheels, and keyboard navigation.
